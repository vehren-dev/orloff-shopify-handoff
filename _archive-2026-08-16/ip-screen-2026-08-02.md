# Charms that are somebody else's property

**2 Aug 2026.** Full visual pass over all **213** named catalogue charms (`ORCB`
prefix), plus one finding in the `ORGCB` group.

Every piece was looked at. The names hide the problem — nobody finds the Superman
shield by searching for "Crowned Shield Charm" — so this was done from the
photographs, not the titles. Contact sheets are in `image-fixes\ip-sheets\`
(11 sheets, 20 per sheet, labelled by SKU) if you want to check my work.

The test applied is **"is the subject somebody's registered or asserted
property"**, not "does anyone else sell it". See the note at the end for why that
distinction is the whole ball game.

---

## Remove. Three of them.

### 1. Crowned Shield Charm — `ORCB180`

The DC Comics Superman shield. Pentagonal diamond, the stylised S, pavé set, with
a crown added on top. The name is a euphemism; the mark itself is unaltered.

Owner: DC Comics / Warner Bros. Discovery. Registered word and device marks in
effectively every market, jewellery included.

### 2. Police Box Charm — `ORCB170`

The TARDIS. Blue box, POLICE across the lintel, lamp on the roof, blue glazed
panes. Not a generic British police box — the proportions and the roof lamp are
the Doctor Who prop.

Owner: the BBC. The BBC applied to register the police box shape in 1996, the
Metropolitan Police opposed, and the UK Patent Office **ruled for the BBC in
2002** — the police had never registered it, and the BBC had been selling
merchandise on it for thirty years. Live registered trademark.

### 3. Puzzle Cube Charm — `ORCB182`

The Rubik's Cube. Coloured enamel squares on a cube.

Owner: Rubik's Brand Ltd. The EU shape mark was invalidated in 2019, so this is
the weakest of the three, but Rubik's holds registrations elsewhere and enforces
on the colour scheme. Not worth the argument for one charm.

## One to decide on

### Best Friends Charm — `ORCB144`

Two yellow smiley faces on a pink heart lettered BFF.

The Smiley Company (Franklin Loufrani) holds the yellow smiley as a trademark in
around a hundred countries, jewellery among the classes, and licenses it
commercially. These are emoji-style faces with blush marks rather than the exact
classic mark, and they do not match Apple's or Google's copyrighted artwork
either. So it is not a clean hit — but it is squarely in the category that company
polices.

Grey. Your call, or a five-minute question to a lawyer.

## The one that matters more, and it is in the group you kept

### Little Mermaid Charm — `ORGCB25`

A silver replica of Edvard Eriksen's statue in Copenhagen, sold commercially, by a
Danish brand.

Eriksen died in 1959. **The copyright runs to 2029.** The estate is among the most
aggressive rights holders in Denmark:

- Won against *Berlingske* over a cartoon and a face-masked photograph.
  300,000 DKK, upheld by the Supreme Court in 2022.
- Went after the village of Asaa over a mermaid statue and demanded
  **demolition**, not just money.
- Pursued a mermaid statue in Michigan.

The right they hold is explicitly the exclusive right to make replicas *and to
depict the statue in other media*. A cast silver miniature sits squarely inside
that. Being Danish makes the exposure worse, not better — it puts you in their
home jurisdiction and makes you an obvious target.

This needs a lawyer's opinion before it goes near a live store. It is the
highest-exposure item in the catalogue, and it was in the keep pile precisely
because it looked like the most Orloff thing on the list.

## Why this list is short, and what "belongs to Gnoce" actually means

Gnoce sells a dachshund charm, a hot air balloon charm and an ice cream charm.
So do you. That does **not** make them Gnoce's designs.

Gnoce was founded in 2015 and buys from Chinese OEMs the same way you buy from
Zhuhai Rinntin. When the same charm appears under Gnoce, on Shopee and in Walmart,
that is one factory selling one mould to four customers. Nobody in that chain owns
the dachshund. Screening on "who else sells it" would flag most of the 213 and
tell you nothing about ownership.

What Gnoce **does** own is its licensed lines — they hold a Peanuts licence, there
is a Woodstock hot-air-balloon charm on their site. That is the category that
produces real infringement, and it is what this sweep was hunting.

**Result: three definite, one grey, out of 213.** The catalogue is generic, not
stolen. That is a different problem from the one you feared, and a smaller one.
