Full site audit · orloffofdenmark.com

A beautifully art-directed storefront that is quietly hiding its own products.

The brand work is genuinely strong and the deep templates — collection and product pages — are better optimised than most jewellery stores. The damage is concentrated in three places: the homepage ships 2.3 MB of unresized imagery, the entire 151-product catalogue sits behind a single nav item labelled "BROWSE", and tag-filtered collections are generating duplicate content against themselves.

01

SEO & Core Web Vitals

There is a sharp split here. Collection and product templates have been carefully worked — unique titles, hand-written meta descriptions, correct canonicals, single H1s, and valid ProductGroup schema carrying all 70 variants with live pricing. The homepage and the tag-filtered pages have had none of that attention.

Titles, meta & URL structure

Critical The homepage title is the brand name and nothing else

<title>Orloff of Denmark</title> — 17 characters against roughly 60 available. It contains no product category, no material, no geography. This is the single highest-authority tag on the domain and it is currently ranking for nothing beyond a brand-name search. The meta description (92 chars) is similarly generic and also under-uses its space.

Fix Something like "Fine Jewellery & Engagement Rings in Gold and Platinum | Orloff of Denmark" — category, material, brand, ~72 chars. Extend the description toward 155 and name the Hua Hin / Bangkok geography that the collection pages already use well.
High Tag-filtered collections are competing with themselves

/collections/wedding-bands/male and /collections/wedding-bands/female each canonicalise to themselves while sharing an identical title, an identical meta description and an identical H1. The same pattern repeats across the Fine Jewelry tag views. Google is being handed several near-identical pages and asked to pick one.

Those titles also leak Shopify's raw separator into search results: Wedding Bands | Orloff of Denmark – Tag.

Fix Either canonicalise tag views to the parent collection, or give each a genuinely unique title, H1 and description ("Men's Wedding Bands", "Women's Wedding Bands"). Strip the "– Tag" suffix from the template either way.

High Four H1s on the homepage; zero on three key pages

The homepage declares H1 four times — SOLAR GROWN DIAMONDS, ENGAGEMENT RINGS, WEDDING BANDS, ONE OF A KIND — because every hero section reuses the same block. Meanwhile /collections/engagement-rings-halo, /pages/diamonds and /pages/about-us have no H1 at all, and /pages/about-diamonds has two.

Fix One H1 per page. On the homepage, promote the first hero to H1 and demote the rest to H2.
Medium Auto-generated meta descriptions on content pages

About Us, Solar Grown Diamonds and About Diamonds all carry 320–324 character descriptions that are plainly the first paragraph of body copy scraped verbatim — one begins "Sunlight in, diamond out The Process A diamond is carbon, pressure, an…". These truncate at roughly 160 characters in search results, mid-sentence.

Structured data

Working well Product schema is correctly implemented

Product pages emit a valid ProductGroup with 70 hasVariant entries, each a full Product with SKU, price, currency, availability and canonical variant URL. Brand is set. This is better than most competitors will have.

Medium Missing breadcrumb, ItemList and review markup

Breadcrumbs render visually on product pages but emit no BreadcrumbList schema, so the breadcrumb rich result is forfeited. Collection pages carry only Organization — no ItemList. And with no review system anywhere on the site there is no aggregateRating, which is the single most visible SERP enhancement in jewellery retail.

Minor but sloppy: the Organization block's sameAs array contains five empty strings alongside the two real social URLs.

Medium Thin product copy across a third of the catalogue

48 of 151 products carry fewer than 150 characters of description; the catalogue average is 307. For a category where buyers research heavily and competitors publish 600–1,000 words on cut, setting, and certification, this is the largest untapped organic-content opportunity on the site.

Core Web Vitals & performance blocks

Critical Full-resolution masters are being served into thumbnail slots

The homepage transfers 3.58 MB across 159 requests, of which 2.26 MB is imagery. The Fine Jewelry tiles are the worst case: four source files at 4096×4096, 3760×3760 and 3568×3532 are rendered into 279×279 tiles on desktop and 164×164 on mobile.

One file, BRACELET_WEBSITE_4.jpg, is 1.22 MB at origin. Requested through Shopify's own CDN as ?width=400&format=webp the identical image is 27.6 KB — a 97.8% reduction, available today with no new tooling and no re-uploading.

Fix Append width and format=webp params and emit a proper srcset on the homepage sections. Expect the page to drop from ~3.6 MB to well under 1 MB.

High No responsive images and almost no lazy-loading on the homepage

Of 19 homepage images, one has a srcset and zero use <picture>. Every file is JPG or PNG — no WebP or AVIF is served anywhere on the page. Only 2 of 19 images are loading="lazy"; the other 17 are eager, so the browser fetches every below-the-fold section image before first paint. Notably, the collection and product templates do use srcset correctly — this is a homepage-specific regression.

High Render-blocking scripts and a duplicated font strategy

Five theme scripts load with neither async nor defer — vendor.min.js, header.min.js, predictive-search.min.js, quantity-input.min.js and app.min.js — each one a parser stall.

Fonts are loaded twice over: Inter self-hosts four separate WOFF2 files (~157 KB) while Cormorant Garamond is pulled from fonts.googleapis.com, a render-blocking third-party stylesheet on the critical path.

Separately, roughly 500 KB of Shopify checkout bundles (hydrate.js 207 KB, useShopPayExternalAppContext 102 KB, OnePage 71 KB) are preloaded on a homepage that has no cart and no checkout.

Fix Add defer to the four non-critical theme scripts, and self-host Cormorant Garamond alongside Inter to remove the third-party font hop entirely.
Medium Layout-shift risk from undimensioned and non-decoding images

Four homepage images carry no width/height attributes, leaving no reserved box and inviting CLS as they arrive. Both header logo <img> elements report an intrinsic size of 0×0 — they never decode — while still occupying a 100×100 slot. On mobile, the Engagement Rings collection banner has an intrinsic size of 375×195 but is painted at 375×890, so it renders visibly upscaled and soft.

Working well Delivery layer is properly configured

Brotli compression is active on HTML, CDN assets carry max-age=31557600, HSTS and a sensible CSP are present, and server-side render time is 27 ms with a 430 ms TTFB from the Bangkok edge. The infrastructure is not the problem — the payload is.

02

Design & Visual Hierarchy

The identity is the strongest asset here. Navy #091b36 with a warm gold accent, a Cormorant Garamond display face against Inter, square-cornered buttons and generous full-bleed photography add up to a coherent, genuinely premium register that suits the category. The problems are consistency and emphasis, not taste.

High The type scale has drifted into 27 variants on a single page

The homepage renders 27 distinct combinations of element, family, weight, size and letter-spacing. Inter appears at four weights (300/400/500/600) across nine sizes — 11, 12, 13, 13.6, 14, 15, 16, 18 and 20 px. The H2 is set at 30 px with three different letter-spacings (−0.15px, −0.3px, normal) and once at 32 px / weight 400 / +1.6px. These are differences a visitor will not name but will feel as looseness.

Fix Collapse to a six-step scale (12 / 14 / 16 / 20 / 30 / 42) with two weights per face, defined once as CSS custom properties and referenced everywhere.
Medium Two golds are in circulation

The computed accent on the live site is #bc973f, while the brand standard recorded for this theme is #b08d57 — a warmer, less saturated tone. Both appear. Pick one and drive it from a single token.

High Every call-to-action has identical visual weight

All eight homepage CTAs — READ, SHOP NOW ×3, SEARCH DIAMONDS, READ — are the same outline treatment: transparent background, 1px border, 0px radius, 13.6px letter-spaced caps. Nothing on the page is styled as the action. The eye has no rank to follow, so it treats an editorial "READ" and a commercial "SHOP NOW" as equals.

Fix Introduce a filled navy primary button for commercial actions and reserve the outline style for secondary/editorial links. One primary per viewport.
Medium A long, evenly-weighted editorial rhythm with no product in it

The homepage runs 6,600 px on desktop and 7,345 px on mobile across 13 content sections that alternate, almost metronomically, between a full-bleed image-with-overlay and a centred rich-text block. Because every section is roughly the same size and the same shape, nothing reads as more important than anything else, and the visitor never encounters a product card, a price, or a piece of social proof. The footer alone is 883 px — 13% of the page.

Fix Break the rhythm: insert a featured-product row with real prices after the engagement hero, and compress two or three of the rich-text interludes.
Working well Restraint in colour and generous whitespace

The palette is disciplined — navy, gold, white, near-black — with no decorative gradients or competing hues. Square button corners and uppercase letter-spaced labels are applied consistently and give the site a recognisable signature.

03

UX & User Journey

Critical The whole catalogue is hidden behind one word: "BROWSE"

The desktop header offers exactly three top-level items: BROWSE, DIAMONDS and ABOUT US. Every one of 151 products, across 19 collections, sits inside the BROWSE mega-menu — which is itself an <a href="#">.

This costs on three fronts at once. Commercially, a first-time visitor sees no shopping entry point without hovering a generic verb. For SEO, the site's most valuable internal anchor text ("Engagement Rings", "Wedding Bands") is demoted a level. For accessibility, a menu trigger built on an empty-fragment link is not a reliable keyboard or screen-reader control.

Fix Promote ENGAGEMENT RINGS, WEDDING BANDS and FINE JEWELRY to top-level nav items in their own right, and rebuild the trigger as a <button> with aria-expanded.
High Two navigation links silently deliver the wrong page

Under Fine Jewelry, RINGS points to /collections/fine-jewelry/rings, which 301s to the unfiltered parent collection — the visitor asks for rings and receives all 24 fine jewellery products with no indication anything was ignored. NECKLACES and PENDANTS are two separate menu entries pointing at the identical URL, and the "necklaces+pendants" variant redirects onward to a 3-product tag page.

High The Add to Cart button is the quietest element on the product page

On /products/katarina-lab-grown the Add to Cart control is rendered with a white background and navy text — visually a secondary or ghost button. It is the single most valuable interaction on the site and it is styled to recede. There is also no sticky Add to Cart on mobile, so on a page of this length the button scrolls away and is never seen again.

High Seventy variants, no guidance, no proof

Each engagement ring exposes 70 variants across five axes — Diamond Origin, Metal, Diamond Grade, Carat Weight and Size — on a page carrying roughly 2,100 characters of total text. A buyer is asked to make five consequential decisions about a $1,000–$35,000 purchase with no explanation of what "Prestige VS1/F" means in that flow.

Compounding it, the page carries no reviews, no ratings, no GIA or certification content, and no warranty or guarantee statement — despite the collection meta description promising "GIA-certified diamonds". The reassurance a high-consideration buyer needs is absent at the exact moment of decision.

Medium A published $0.00 product with no images

/products/micro is live and indexable with a price of $0.00, zero images and no SKU. It is not purchasable — the variant is marked unavailable, so there is no checkout exposure — but it is a thin, empty page in the index, and it drags the Engagement Rings price filter down to read "$0.00 — $35,630.00", which undermines the price framing on a luxury collection page.

Fix Archive or unpublish it. This appears to be a legacy duplicate of MICRO ETERNITY BAND.

04

Mobile Responsiveness & Accessibility

Working well Layout integrity at 375px is sound

No horizontal overflow: scrollWidth equals the 375px viewport exactly, and the off-canvas panels are correctly parked off-screen rather than leaking. The viewport meta is properly formed and permits zoom to 5×. Adjacent tap targets are not crowded — zero overlapping-pair violations were measured.

Critical The gold accent fails WCAG AA everywhere it is used as a label

Gold #bc973f on white measures 2.75:1 against a required 4.5:1 — and it is used at 12px, the size most in need of contrast. This affects every section eyebrow on the homepage: Solar Grown, Philosophy, Eternity, Craftsmanship, Rarity, Heritage.

Fix Darken the label gold to roughly #7a6129 (≈4.6:1) for small text, and keep the brighter gold for large display type and non-text ornament only.
High 32 touch targets below the 44×44 minimum

Measured at a 375px viewport. The footer is the main offender — every link there (Sizing, Returns, Shipping, Privacy Policy, Terms, the two email addresses and both phone numbers) is 20px tall. The two social icons are 18×18, the cart link 39×50, the newsletter consent checkbox 13×13, and the skip link 24px. Hero CTAs sit exactly on the 44px line, which passes but leaves no margin.

Fix Add vertical padding to footer anchors to reach a 44px hit area — the visual type size need not change at all.
High Thirteen homepage images carry an empty alt attribute

No image is missing alt outright — but 13 of 19 are set to alt="", which declares them purely decorative. Several of those are the hero and section images that carry the actual editorial meaning of the page. A screen-reader user gets the headline and nothing else. Product and collection templates, by contrast, have genuine descriptive alt text ("Katarina Engagement Ring") — again a homepage-specific gap.

Medium Unlabelled controls and a very long mobile scroll

Two icon-only buttons expose no accessible name, and two links resolve to href="#". Body copy is set in Inter 300 — a light weight at 16px that, while it passes contrast at roughly 8:1, is thinner than ideal for extended reading on a phone. Six text runs fall below 14px. The homepage itself is 7,345px of mobile scroll before the footer.

05

Priority Action Plan

Ranked by impact per hour of work. The first three are performance and SEO; the last two are UX and design. All five are achievable inside a single sprint on the draft theme.

1
SEO / Performance ~2 hours

Resize and re-format every homepage image through the Shopify CDN

Append width and format=webp parameters, emit srcset for the tile and hero sections, and set loading="lazy" on the 17 images currently eager. The collection template already does this correctly — the markup can be lifted directly.

Expected result: homepage payload falls from 3.58 MB toward roughly 0.9 MB. The single worst asset drops from 1.22 MB to 27.6 KB. This is the largest available Core Web Vitals gain on the site by a wide margin.

2
SEO ~30 minutes

Write a real homepage title and fix the H1 structure

Replace the 17-character brand-only title with a keyword-bearing one, extend the meta description to ~155 characters, promote a single hero to H1 and demote the other three to H2. While in the template, add an H1 to the Halo collection, Diamonds and About Us pages, and remove the duplicate on About Diamonds.

Expected result: the homepage becomes eligible to rank for category terms it currently cannot compete for at all. Highest ratio of impact to effort in this list.

3
SEO ~1 hour

Resolve the tag-page duplicate content and strip the "– Tag" suffix

Point tag-filtered collection views at the parent canonical, or give each a unique title, H1 and description. Remove the literal "– Tag" string from the title template. Then archive the published $0.00 /products/micro page, which also repairs the "$0.00 — $35,630.00" price filter on the Engagement Rings collection.

Expected result: consolidated ranking signals on wedding-band and fine-jewellery terms, and cleaner SERP snippets.

4
UX ~3 hours

Surface the catalogue in the navigation and fix the broken category links

Promote ENGAGEMENT RINGS, WEDDING BANDS and FINE JEWELRY to top-level header items instead of hiding all 151 products behind "BROWSE". Rebuild the mega-menu trigger as a <button> with aria-expanded rather than href="#". Repoint the Fine Jewelry RINGS link so it stops redirecting to the unfiltered parent, and merge the duplicate NECKLACES / PENDANTS entries.

Expected result: shorter path to product for every visitor, stronger internal anchor text, and a keyboard-accessible menu.

5
Design / Accessibility ~2 hours

Establish a CTA hierarchy and fix the gold contrast

Make Add to Cart a filled navy button — it is currently white-on-navy ghost styling — and add a sticky mobile variant. Introduce one filled primary per viewport on the homepage and leave the rest as outline. In the same pass, darken the small-text gold from #bc973f (2.75:1, failing) to about #7a6129, and pad footer links to a 44px hit area.

Expected result: a clear visual path to conversion, plus WCAG AA compliance on the two most widespread accessibility failures.
