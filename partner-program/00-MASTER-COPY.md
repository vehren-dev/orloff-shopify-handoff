# PART 0: CLAIMS RULES (internal, never published)

These govern every asset. They exist because the Empowering Consumers Directive
(EU 2024/825) applies from 27 September 2026, the same window as this launch, 
and because Konsumentombudsmannen actively pursues green claims in Sweden.

### Always

- First mention on every asset: **"Solar Grown lab-grown diamonds"**. "Solar Grown"
  is an adjective describing *how* they are grown. It never replaces the words
  "lab-grown". Abbreviating to "Solar Grown diamonds" alone reads as a growing
  method rather than a synthetic origin, which is exactly the pattern the FTC
  Jewelry Guides and EU consumer law treat as misleading.
- State the solar share as a number, with its mechanism (on-site PV / PPA / RECs).
- Say what the certificate is and who issues it.
- "Recycled gold" always paired with "with chain-of-custody documentation".

### Never

- No superlatives about environmental performance. Not "the most", not "one of
  the most", not "the world's most". These are comparative claims requiring
  substantiation against competitors you cannot audit.
- No bare "sustainable", "eco-friendly", "green", "climate-neutral", "planet-
  friendly" as standalone adjectives about the company or collection.
- No "carbon neutral" or offset-based claims unless you hold the certification.
- "Sustainable beech wood" → "FSC-certified beech wood" if certified, otherwise
  just "beech wood". The adjective needs a standard behind it.

### The substitution that does the work

> ✗ "what we believe is one of the world's most environmentally responsible
>    luxury jewellery collections"
>
> ✓ "Every element documented: diamonds grown on [X]% solar energy, 18K gold from
>    certified recycled sources with chain-of-custody documentation, beech
>    presentation box, linen interior. Documentation supplied with every ring."

Specific and verifiable is both safer *and* more persuasive to a trade buyer.
A jeweller discounts superlatives automatically; a jeweller cannot discount a number.

### Note on #9, certificates

GIA moved lab-grown diamond reports to descriptive categories rather than the
D-to-Z colour and Flawless-to-I clarity scales used for natural stones. IGI
continues to issue full grades. A jeweller who reads "GIA certified" will expect
4Cs and may be surprised by what the customer actually receives. Confirm current
practice with both labs, then state precisely which report accompanies each ring
and what it shows. This is a selling point when handled well and a trust problem
when handled vaguely.

### Note on #12, landed cost

The offer is priced in USD (changed from SEK on 4 Aug 2026), matching the store's
own currency. That removes one layer of friction but shifts exchange-rate risk
onto the buyer: a Nordic jeweller earns in kronor and now carries the USD swing
between quote and payment. Two consequences worth planning for:

- **Quote validity.** State how long a USD price holds. "prices valid 30 days"
  is standard and protects you both. Without it, every krona move becomes a
  renegotiation.
- **The comparison problem.** A Swedish retailer comparing you against a European
  supplier quoting in EUR or SEK has to do mental arithmetic to see your offer.
  Consider showing an indicative SEK figure alongside, marked as indicative.

Separately, on landed cost: goods ship from Thailand to the EU and there is no
free trade agreement between the EU and Thailand, so articles of precious metal attract the
standard third-country duty rate plus import VAT (25% in Sweden, reclaimable for
VAT-registered businesses but a real cash-flow item). A Nordic jeweller *will*
ask whether 2 600 USD is the landed cost or the pre-clearance cost. Answer it in
the FAQ before it becomes an objection in the reply email.
