Why nothing uploaded

This was previously recorded as a permission prompt that could be retried. That was wrong, and the distinction matters because it changes who can fix it.

Attempting to write the privacy policy returns this, from Shopify rather than from the client:

Access denied for shopPolicyUpdate field. Required access: write_legal_policies access scope.

The connected app does not hold the write_legal_policies scope. No retry will succeed, and no permission rule on this end changes it. Policies are the one part of the store that cannot be reached from here at all.

Which leaves two routes

  1. Paste them yourself. Every policy below is written out in full with a copy button. Shopify admin, Settings, Policies, paste, save. Perhaps fifteen minutes for all four.
  2. Grant the scope. If the app can be reauthorised with write_legal_policies, I can write all four directly and you review them on the live pages instead.
Copying into Shopify

The copy button puts formatted HTML on the clipboard, so headings and bold survive the paste into Shopify's rich text editor. If your browser blocks the clipboard, the button selects the text instead and you press Ctrl+C.

Paste into the editor's normal view, not the <> HTML view.

Privacy policy

Written to cover Thailand's PDPA and EU or UK GDPR together, since you sell from Thailand into Europe. It describes your current data footprint, which is unusually small because you run no third-party trackers today. Revisit it the day you install one.

Draft, replaces the empty policy entirely

Last updated 16 August 2026.

Orloff of Denmark (Thailand) Co., Ltd operates orloffofdenmark.com. This policy explains what personal data we collect, why we hold it, and what you can require of us.

Who is responsible. Orloff of Denmark (Thailand) Co., Ltd, 146/51 Natural Hill 2, Hin Lek Fai, Hua Hin, Prachuap Khiri Khan 77110, Thailand. Write to info@orloffofdenmark.com about anything in this policy.

What we collect

When you order: your name, billing and delivery address, email address, telephone number, what you bought, and any ring size or engraving instructions you give us. Card details are entered directly with our payment providers. We never see or store a full card number.

When you browse: your IP address, device and browser type, the pages you view and the site you arrived from. This comes from our store platform's own cookies.

When you write to us or subscribe: your email address and whatever you choose to put in the message.

Why we hold it

  • To take, make, ship and support your order, because we have a contract with you.
  • To meet tax, accounting, export and customs obligations, because the law requires it.
  • To prevent fraud and keep the store secure, because we have a legitimate interest in doing so.
  • To send marketing email, only if you have consented, and only until you withdraw.
  • To understand how the store is used, with your consent where cookies require it.

Email we send you

There are two kinds, and they are treated differently.

Order email, meaning confirmations, dispatch and delivery notices, and messages about a piece being made for you, is part of fulfilling your order. Every customer receives it. It is not marketing.

Marketing email, meaning new pieces, offers and private viewings, is sent only to people who have asked for it. Every one carries an unsubscribe link, and you can also write to info@orloffofdenmark.com.

Some offers are limited to a single country, or to our storefront in Hua Hin. Where that is the case we choose recipients by the country of the address you have given us, so that we do not send you an invitation you cannot use. We do not build profiles of you beyond your location and what you have bought from us.

Cookies

Necessary cookies keep your basket and your chosen language and currency working. The store cannot function without them and they are always on.

Analytics cookies tell us which pages are visited and how people move through the store. They are set by our store platform.

Marketing cookies would let us measure advertising and show you relevant ads. We set these only if you agree, and you can change your mind at any time from the cookie settings in the footer.

Who else sees it

Our store and checkout platform; our payment providers; the carriers who deliver your parcel and the customs authorities they must declare it to; our email provider; our accountants and auditors; and any authority we are legally obliged to answer. We do not sell personal data and we never have.

Sending data abroad

We operate from Thailand and our suppliers are spread across Europe, the United States and Asia, so your data will cross borders. Where we move data out of the EEA or the UK we rely on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses, or on an adequacy decision where one exists.

How long we keep it

  • Order, invoice and customs records: ten years, because tax law requires it.
  • Marketing consent: until you withdraw it, then a further three years as evidence that you did.
  • Correspondence with us: three years.
  • Analytics: no more than twenty-four months.

What you can require of us

You may ask for a copy of your data, ask us to correct it, ask us to delete it, object to what we are doing with it, ask us to restrict it, ask for it in a portable form, and withdraw any consent you have given. Write to info@orloffofdenmark.com and we will answer within thirty days.

If we get it wrong you can complain. In the EU or UK, to your national data protection authority. In Thailand, to the Office of the Personal Data Protection Committee.

Children

This store is not intended for children under sixteen and we do not knowingly collect their data.

Changes

The date at the top of this page is the date it last changed. If we change anything that materially affects you, we will email everyone who has subscribed to hear from us.

Confirm before this goes live

Ten-year retention. That follows Thai accounting practice. If your accountant works to a different period, say so and I will change it.

Registered entity name. The contact policy says Orloff of Denmark (Thailand) Co., Ltd. If a separate company is registered in the EU, the controller section needs both.

EU representative. Selling into the EU from outside it can require a named Article 27 representative. One question to your lawyer. If you need one, they get named here.

Where we ship from

Every order is dispatched from our workshop in Hua Hin, Thailand, wherever in the world you are.

Rates

Regular shipping is free. Express shipping is $32. Courier shipping is $54. Every parcel is tracked, and you receive the tracking number when it leaves us.

Four blanks I refused to invent

This draft is publishable as it stands and is a large improvement on four lines of prices. It is not finished, because four facts are yours alone to supply.

Dispatch time. Stated nowhere on the site. Configured rings are made to order, so what is it: five working days, three weeks? This is the single most asked pre-purchase question in fine jewellery.

Delivery windows. What do regular, express and courier mean in days, to Europe and to the US?

Insurance. Are parcels insured to full value in transit, and up to what amount? The customs paragraph above already asserts that insurance exists.

DDP. Some jewellers pay duties on the customer's behalf and price it in. Worth considering. A surprise customs bill on a $7,000 ring is how chargebacks start.

Cookie consent

This has to be fixed before any tracking goes in, not after. Right now the banner is worse than having none, because it claims a consent process that does not exist.

What it does today

I read the live snippet on 16 August. It is 735 bytes and contains exactly one button.

  • Offers "I Accept" and nothing else. No reject, no preferences.
  • Never calls Shopify.customerPrivacy. The API is not loaded on the page at all, so nothing is gated by the answer.
  • Links to a privacy policy that is currently blank.

The fix

Do not rebuild the theme banner. Shopify ships a consent banner wired into the Customer Privacy API natively. It updates itself as rules change and applies region by region, so you are not showing a GDPR banner to Thai customers who do not need one.

Enable it in Settings, Customer privacy, Cookie banner, then delete snippets/cookie-notice.liquid so the two cannot both appear. It is still rendered from layout/theme.liquid, so the deletion is a real step, not a tidy-up.

Why the order matters

Every tracking tool in the next section installs as a Shopify web pixel. Web pixels run sandboxed and are gated automatically by the consent API, but only if something is actually setting consent.

Install the pixels first and they fire for everyone regardless of choice, which is the specific thing that draws enforcement.

Analytics and pixels

The order is deliberate. Each step depends on the one before it. The rule throughout: install through Shopify's official channel apps, never by pasting tags into theme code.

  1. Consent banner live

    From the section above. Everything downstream is gated by it, so nothing else starts until it is on and you have watched a real visit set a consent value.

  2. Google Analytics 4

    Install through the Google and YouTube channel, not by pasting a gtag snippet. The channel wires GA4 to Shopify's consent mode automatically, so denied consent downgrades to cookieless pings instead of silently tracking anyway. It also carries purchase events from checkout, which a theme-pasted tag cannot reach.

    With zero completed checkouts in thirty days, and in fact zero orders of any kind since 14 July, this is the one that earns its keep first. It will show you where in the funnel people stop.

  3. Klaviyo

    Different in kind from the ad pixels, and the one I would do second. This is your email platform: newsletter signup, abandoned cart flows, post-purchase sequences.

    It also solves the segmentation you asked about. Build one list, then segment by the country on the customer's address, so a Hua Hin storefront event only reaches Thai customers while a new-collection announcement goes to everyone. Keep marketing consent as its own property so unsubscribing from promotions never silences order email.

  4. Meta pixel

    Install via the Facebook and Instagram channel. Turn on the Conversions API alongside the browser pixel: with iOS tracking prevention, browser-only signal is roughly half the picture. Only worth doing when you are actually spending on Meta ads. An idle pixel collects data you then have to justify holding.

  5. TikTok

    Same pattern, via the TikTok channel. I would defer this one. It is the weakest fit for a considered, high-price purchase, and every additional pixel widens what your privacy policy has to disclose and defend.

The numerals

You did not need a new typeface. Cormorant was drawing oldstyle figures, numerals that vary in height and drop below the baseline, designed to sit inside flowing lowercase text. In a tracked-out uppercase heading they look like a mistake.

There is exactly one place on the site where a Cormorant numeral appears: the homepage heading SINCE 1995, at 34px. Every other number you see, prices, carat weights, metal grades, is already set in Inter, whose figures are perfectly ordinary.

Cormorant carries lining figures as an alternate, so the fix is one declaration.

Wasfont-variant-numeric: normal
Oldstyle figures. The 9s descend, the 1 is short.
Nowfont-variant-numeric: lining-nums
Full-height figures aligned to the caps.

I verified the alternate exists rather than assuming it: rendering the digits with and without the feature produces measurably different glyph widths, so the font genuinely contains both sets.

Deliberately not font-feature-settings: 'lnum'. That low-level property replaces the whole feature list and would switch off ligatures and kerning at the same time.

Applied, this one is done

Live in theme WEB 19 as assets/orloff-numerals.css. The heading computes lining-nums and "1995" measures 181.41px against the old 167.11px at 100px, so the glyphs really changed.

It is a separate file rather than an eighth section of orloff-fixes.css on purpose. Two agents rewriting one 13KB file in the same hour is how work gets silently overwritten.

WEB 19 is unpublished, so it is not yet customer-facing.

If you still want to change the face

Then do it for a better reason than the numbers, because the numbers are fixed in place. Cormorant's real liabilities are its very light strokes at small sizes and the slanted hyphen we already had to patch.

The serifs that keep a Garamond feeling with sturdier figures are EB Garamond, Spectral and Lora. All three are on Google Fonts, so read the next section first.

Google Fonts

Cormorant is loaded from fonts.gstatic.com. That transmits every visitor's IP address to Google before they have consented to anything, which is the practice German courts have already ruled against under GDPR.

I confirmed the exact request in the live theme's layout/theme.liquid on 16 August:

family=Cormorant+Garamond:ital,wght@0,400;0,500;0,600;1,400;1,500

Five faces: 400, 500 and 600 upright, plus 400 and 500 italic.

The real fix is to self-host properly, an hour rather than a minute. Download the five Cormorant Garamond .woff2 files, upload them as theme assets, declare them with @font-face pointing at asset_url, and only then remove the Google Fonts link.

Done that way it removes the GDPR exposure, removes two DNS lookups and a render-blocking request, and makes the page faster. It also simplifies the hyphen patch, since self-hosted faces can be subset before upload. Worth scheduling alongside the consent banner rather than squeezing into it.

Correction to an earlier draft of this page

I first wrote that this fix was free because Shopify already serves Cormorant from your own domain. That was too quick and it was wrong.

Shopify hosts exactly one face: cormorant_n5.woff2, weight 500 upright, under the family name Cormorant. Your headings use Cormorant Garamond at three weights plus italics, and all five come from Google.

Dropping the link today would silently lose four of the five faces and fall back to a system serif. Do not do it yet.
