## Yours, but not on the store (24 designs)

### Worth naming and launching

- **`GCB18` — Cambodian flag.** Angkor Wat in silver relief on red and blue

- **`GCB11`, `GCB12`** — figures in gold and silver, 27–30mm. Unnamed.

  metal as the Waves ring. This belongs with the fine jewelry, not in a charm
  folder.

### The set that needs a decision that is not mine (22 SKUs)

`GCB19` through `GCB30`, each in silver (`-P`) and 18K (`-18K`):

Buddha heads, seated Buddhas, standing deities, temple lions, and `GCB22` — the
**Garuda**, which is the state emblem of Thailand.

Two problems, and they are not legal ones:

1. **Buddha images as decorative jewellery are actively campaigned against in
   Thailand.** The government has run public campaigns on exactly this point. A
   Bangkok-based brand selling Buddha head charms is taking a reputational risk
   in its home market that a foreign brand would not.
2. **The Garuda is a restricted state emblem.** Already flagged on 31 Jul as
   possibly not commercially usable at all.

These are eleven designs and twenty-two SKUs — a third of everything you own.
They were held back for a reason and the reason still holds.

## What this changes

You said you would not put the name on stock anyone can buy. On this evidence you
do not have to. There are 36 commissioned designs, and if Rinntin confirms
exclusivity they are a real collection: two Danish flags, a Cambodian flag, the
coat of arms, the royal guard, the Little Mermaid, the lotus, the tassel, the
whale tail, the durian, a fine silver bangle.

That is a Denmark-meets-South-East-Asia line with a story nobody can buy from a
catalogue. It is small, which is a feature.

The 216 catalogue charms are then a separate commercial question — sell them
somewhere else, sell them honestly as compatible accessories, or do not sell them.
But they should not be what the Orloff charm line *is*.
